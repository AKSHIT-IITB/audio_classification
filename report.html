A note on the data

There's no licensed music corpus available in this environment, so the corpus was synthesized: 120 three-second clips across four genres (classical, electronic, rock, jazz) × three “artists” each, built from genuinely distinct synthesis recipes — additive harmonics with ADSR envelopes for classical, saw-wave arpeggios over a four-on-the-floor kick for electronic, distorted power chords with drum hits for rock, vibrato tone over a swung rhythm for jazz.

This keeps every figure on this page real and reproducible — running python3 run_pipeline.py regenerates identical numbers. The pipeline logic itself (feature extraction, GA selection, K-Means, centroid classification, evaluation) is unchanged from what you'd run on a real corpus like GTZAN — only the audio source differs.

01

Audio → MFCC features

Each clip is reduced to 75 numbers: 13 MFCCs and 13 delta-MFCCs (mean + std across frames), spectral centroid / bandwidth / rolloff, zero-crossing rate, RMS energy, a 12-bin chroma profile, and tempo. MFCCs dominate the set because they approximate how the human ear perceives timbre — the trait that most separates one genre's instrumentation from another's.

02

Genetic algorithm feature selection

A binary-chromosome GA (population 40, 60 generations, tournament selection, uniform crossover, bit-flip mutation, elitism 2) searches subsets of the 75 features. Fitness is the silhouette score of a K-Means(k=4) clustering on the training set minus a small penalty per feature used — genre labels are never involved, so the search stays unsupervised.

04

Classifying unlabeled clips

The 60 held-out clips are scaled with the training scaler, then assigned to whichever of the 4 centroids is nearest by Euclidean distance — the “least centroid distance” method.

● 60 / 60 correct
Why 100%, and what it does & doesn't show

The four synthetic genres come from cleanly distinct signal recipes, so their MFCC fingerprints barely overlap — this confirms the pipeline mechanics are wired correctly end-to-end, not that K-Means “solves” genre classification. On a real corpus like GTZAN, where genres genuinely blend, expect a realistic 55–75% range instead.

05

t-SNE visualization

t-SNE projects the feature space to 2D purely for visual inspection — it plays no part in training or classification. True genre labels (left) and predicted clusters (right) are plotted separately so agreement between them can be seen, not assumed.

06

The 30-second version

“The project converts raw audio into MFCC-based numerical feature vectors, since MFCCs capture the spectral characteristics of sound the way humans perceive it. Beyond MFCCs I also computed spectral, rhythm, and delta features — about 75 per clip. A genetic algorithm searched subsets of those features, using clustering quality as the fitness function, and narrowed it to 12 that gave tighter, better-separated clusters. I trained K-Means on the resulting vectors, then classified new, unlabeled clips by distance to the nearest cluster centroid — and evaluated everything with silhouette scores and t-SNE to confirm the clusters were real, not a metric artifact.”

Full source, data, and a one-command reproduction (python3 run_pipeline.py) live in music_classificaiton/ — see README.md and RESULTS.md.
